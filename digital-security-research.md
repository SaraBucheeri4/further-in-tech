# Did my operator ask for this?

Research notes for the Further in Tech AI Challenge 2026 · 21 November 2026

## The product decision

Keep the Chrome extension and mobile experience. Give both one job. When a person claiming to be telecom support asks the customer to do something, let the customer check the request with their operator and finish any legitimate task inside official support. The customer can use this even when the incoming message looks completely convincing.

Track 03 covers fraudulent calls, messages and fake support accounts. The brief weights innovation and telecom relevance at 25% each, AI at 20%, feasibility at 15% and presentation/demo at 15%. It calls for a working technology prototype. Bahrain is the reference market for this research. The design assumes one willing operator and can be demonstrated with a fictional one.

## Why it fits Bahrain

The TRA and Bahrain’s mobile operators have published guidance covering SMS detection, blocking, user education and reporting. The Information & eGovernment Authority has also documented fraudulent messages using the familiar names eKey and eGovBahrain. Genuine messages use those names too. Familiar wording alone gives a customer weak evidence. [TRA announcement](https://www.tra.org.bh/en/en/article/tra-bahrain-and-mobile-operators-launch-guidelines-to-combat-fraudulent-sms) · [iGA advisory](https://www.iga.gov.bh/en/article/iga-warns-against-responding-to-scam-text-messages-using-official-terms-claiming-to-be-ekey-and-egovbahrain)

Batelco has documented callers impersonating its staff and has published independent ways to reach support. Its code of practice also explains that genuine disconnection warnings can arrive by SMS. A blanket rule that calls every urgent service message fraudulent would therefore make a poor local demo. Show both a real service notice and a fake one. [Batelco impersonation advisory](https://www.batelco.com/archive/batelco-warns-customers-to-be-on-the-alert-for-fraudulent-calls-promising-cash-prizes-3/) · [Batelco customer support and code of practice](https://www.batelco.com/support/)

## Three directions worth pitching

| Idea | Signature moment | Main dependency |
|---|---|---|
| **Did you ask me?** | A customer really does have an open support case. Someone using the case as a pretext asks for a login code. The operator app shows the actual request was only to arrange a callback. The customer continues there. | One operator makes recent support actions available to its authenticated customer. Use mock records on stage. |
| **Take me to real support** | A student shares a social support profile or opens its web page. The tool checks the exact account handle against the operator’s official directory and opens the genuine support channel in one tap. | A maintained directory of official accounts. Easiest scope for a short build. It helps even without access to customer records. |
| **Rehearse the call** | A user chooses Arabic or English and tries a one-minute simulated support conversation. The user can interrupt and practice independently contacting the provider. | Validated local examples and testing with people of different ages. Good education angle. Less immediate protection during a real incident. |

## The first prototype

1. Use a fictional operator, one test subscriber and two visibly similar examples. The first contact accurately describes a scheduled callback. The second refers to the same real case and asks the user to read out a login code.
2. In mobile, accept pasted text or a user-selected screenshot. For a call, let the user tap **Someone says they’re support** and select what they were asked to do. This works even when the caller uses a private number. Give typed and spoken output equal prominence.
3. Extract the claimed organisation, requested action and relevant phrase locally. Test a real on-device model on an English example, an Arabic example and an Arabic-English mixed message before choosing it for the demo. When extraction is uncertain, let the customer select the intended action. Mask codes. The model has no authority to declare a contact genuine.
4. Have the operator record specify the subscriber, case, channel, time window and permitted action. For a production lookup, a signed-in customer retrieves their own recent actions. The raw message, screenshot and call audio stay off the operator server. Offline, show local findings and the time of the last update. A fresh fact about a live call or account requires current operator evidence.
5. In desktop Chrome, interrupt a fictional support page before form submission. Explain the specific discrepancy and offer **Open official support**. Load the destination from the product’s own directory. For a social account hosted on a legitimate platform, compare the exact handle or profile URL. The platform’s domain alone tells you little about the account.
6. Demonstrate three results: a matching action that can be completed inside official support, a request outside the recorded purpose, and insufficient evidence. A genuine open case never authenticates the person in an incoming call or DM. Make official support accessible in all three states.

**Platform scope.** Apple’s message filter covers SMS and MMS from unknown senders, with contacts and iMessage outside its scope. Call Directory supports identification and blocking by number. Android provides a user-selected call-screening role. Google Play restricts SMS and call-log permissions to qualifying uses. A desktop Chrome extension can act on permitted sites. The mobile share/paste flow and a simulated call let the team demonstrate the value without depending on privileged access. [Apple message filtering](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/identitylookup/sms-and-mms-message-filtering) · [Apple call identification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/identifying-and-blocking-calls) · [Android call screening](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telecom/CallScreeningService) · [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241) · [Chrome permissions](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/declare-permissions)

## What to test before pitching

Give a younger and an older tester the same pair of contacts without coaching. Ask each to handle them as they normally would. Record whether they avoid disclosing the code, whether they still complete the genuine support task, how long it takes to reach the official channel and which words they find confusing. Include a third scenario where the operator record is unavailable. Report actual counts with the number of participants, and describe any projected impact separately.

**Question for a telecom mentor:** Can the outbound-support system tell the customer’s signed-in app which action was authorised, through which channel, and until when? If the system only records a ticket number, start the pilot with scheduled callbacks, where the provider controls the event end to end.
